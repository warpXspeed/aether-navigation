# Open Questions and Theoretical Burdens

## Purpose
An honest framework must explicitly map its known gaps, unverified mathematical extensions, and areas where empirical testing is most urgently required. This document catalogs the current theoretical burdens of the Scalar Relaxation Cosmology (SRC) and Dynamic Fluid Medium (DFM) framework.

## 1. Mathematical Scaling of Finite-Gap Riemann Surfaces
- **The Burden:** While finite-gap compact Riemann surfaces and Riemann theta functions provide a robust topological language for wave dynamics, scaling these equations from microscopic condensate behaviors to macroscopic planetary/interstellar medium interactions remains computationally heavy.
- **Path Forward:** Expanding the Python engine (`dfm_engine`) to handle multi-scale boundary conditions without losing phase-coherence tracking.

## 2. Threshold Limits for Phase Synchronization
- **The Burden:** The exact energy threshold required to induce vacuum refractive index ($n_{\text{aether}}$) shifts via neutral collective mode synchronization has not yet been empirically measured.
- **Path Forward:** Controlled laboratory benchtop testing of resonant cavity oscillators against high-precision interferometers (Phase 1 testing).

## 3. Core Discharge Thermodynamics
- **The Burden:** Modeling the exact feedback loop between planetary core quantum-mechanical macro-batteries and the broader aether substrate requires better empirical constraints on telluric current variations.
- **Path Forward:** Correlating high-precision gravitational constant ($G$) scatter data with global telluric and plasma flux monitors.

## 4. Material Fatigue under High-Frequency Plasma Sheaths
- **The Burden:** The long-term structural integrity of multi-layered gradient hull materials under continuous megahertz-gigahertz Lorentz/Eddy current modulation is theoretically unverified.
- **Path Forward:** Accelerated thermal-acoustic and plasma-bombardment stress testing on small-scale composite coupons.
