# 02 - Dynamic Fluid Medium (DFM) Model

## 2.1 Rejection of Static Spacetime
General Relativity treats spacetime as a passive, elastic geometric fabric warped by mass-energy. The Dynamic Fluid Medium (DFM) model rejects this abstraction. Spacetime geometry is an emergent macroscopic symptom, not a fundamental cause. 

Under Scalar Relaxation Cosmology (SRC), the universe is underpinned by an active, history-preserving, flowing aether substrate. Gravitational and electromagnetic phenomena are fluid-dynamic manifestations of this substrate interacting with quantum-mechanical collective states (planetary cores).

## 2.2 Mathematical Formalization of the Substrate
The DFM engine models wave dynamics and field propagation through finite-gap compact Riemann surfaces. Rather than differential equations operating on static Euclidean or pseudo-Riemannian manifolds, the state of the medium is tracked via Riemann theta functions:

$$\theta(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{\Omega}) = \sum_{\mathbf{n} \in \mathbb{Z}^g} \exp\left(2\pi i \left(\frac{1}{2} \mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{\Omega} \mathbf{n} + \mathbf{n}^T \mathbf{z}\right)\right)$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{z}$ represents the phase coordinates driven by local plasma and rotational fluxes.
- $\mathbf{\Omega}$ is the period matrix encoding the global topology of the aether substrate.

## 2.3 Adiabatic Moduli Drift
The local "density" and resistance of the aether are not uniform. They are governed by **path-dependent adiabatic moduli drift**. 
- As planetary bodies traverse the medium, their core discharges inject structured pulses into the aether.
- These pulse injections modify the local compressibility and refractive index of the DFM.
- Orbital capture and satellite transitions are not ballistic trajectories dictated by an invisible attractive force; they are natural drift paths along variable-density gradients within the flowing medium—analogous to fluid dynamic drafting or vortex capture in hydrodynamics.

## 2.4 Aether Memory
Because the substrate preserves historical phase information through finite-gap deformations, planetary systems retain an energetic "memory" of their capture stages and evolutionary trajectory. The DFM model accounts for this by integrating historical pulse injections into the baseline field equations, explaining anomalous orbital stability and non-Newtonian rotational curves without resorting to invisible "dark matter" constructs.
